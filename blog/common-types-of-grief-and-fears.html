Kinds of Loss

Common Types of Grief and the Fears That Come With Them

The Murido TeamJul 13, 2026 · 3 min read

Every loss brings its own set of fears. Understanding the fears linked to your kind of loss can help you feel less alone and find the right support.

Losing a parent

Common fears: being unanchored, losing the family center, forgetting their voice, facing your own aging. Many adults feel like orphans at any age.

Losing a partner

Common fears: being alone, managing the household, losing your identity as part of a couple, and whether it is ever acceptable to love again.

Losing a child

Common fears: that the grief will never be survivable, that you failed to protect them, that others will forget them. Parents often fear their child's name will stop being said.

Losing a sibling

Common fears: losing the person who shared your whole history, being overlooked while attention goes to parents, and carrying family memories alone.

Losing a friend

Common fears: that your grief does not count because you were not family, and losing a part of yourself that only they knew.

Losing a pet

Common fears: that others will think you are overreacting, and guilt over decisions such as euthanasia.

Sudden loss

Common fears: replaying the moment, never getting answers, and the world feeling unsafe.

Loss after illness

Common fears: guilt over relief, wondering whether you did enough, and memories of their suffering crowding out the rest.

What these fears share

Underneath most of these is a single fear: that the person and your love for them will fade. That is why preserving memories, telling stories, and staying connected matter so much.

Finding your people

Many grief groups focus on specific losses, such as parents who lost children or widowed partners. Being with people who share your kind of loss can be powerful. If fear ever becomes despair, call or text 988.
