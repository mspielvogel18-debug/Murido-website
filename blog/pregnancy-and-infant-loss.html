Pregnancy and Infant Loss: Grieving a Baby

July 19, 2026 · 3 min read · The Murido Team

Losing a pregnancy or a baby is one of the most painful and least acknowledged losses. Parents may grieve not just a baby but a whole imagined future: the name, the nursery, the first steps, the person they would have become.

A real loss

Whether it happened at eight weeks or after birth, this is a real loss. Many parents feel that others do not understand the depth of their grief, especially if there was no funeral or few people knew about the pregnancy.

Common feelings

  • Deep longing for the baby
  • Guilt and searching for something you did wrong, even when nothing could have changed the outcome
  • Envy or pain around pregnant people and babies
  • Physical reminders, such as milk coming in or postpartum changes, that add to grief
  • Strain between partners who grieve differently

Partners grieve too

Non-birthing partners often feel overlooked, with attention focused on the person who was pregnant. They may hide their grief to be strong. Both partners deserve space to mourn.

Ways to remember

  • Naming the baby, if you have not already
  • Keeping a scan photo, hospital bracelet, or footprint
  • Writing a letter to your baby
  • Marking the due date or birth date each year
  • Creating a memory book for the hopes you held

Subsequent pregnancies

A later pregnancy can bring joy alongside fear and renewed grief. It does not replace the baby who died. Many parents find it helpful to talk openly about both.

Getting support

There are support organizations and groups specifically for pregnancy and infant loss. A doctor can also check for postpartum depression or anxiety, which can occur after any pregnancy loss. If you have thoughts of harming yourself, call or text 988 any time.
