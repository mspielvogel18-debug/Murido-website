Willingness vs. Willpower: Why You Can't Force Your Way Through Grief

The Murido TeamMay 11, 2026 · 3 min read

A lot of grieving people approach their loss the way they approach everything else hard in life: by gritting their teeth. Stay busy. Do not cry at work. Get back to normal. That works for some tasks. Grief is not one of them.

Why willpower runs out

Willpower is useful for getting through a hard afternoon. As a long-term strategy for grief, it tends to fail because grief is not a task with an end point. Holding feelings down takes constant effort, and that effort is exhausting. Many people who power through for months eventually find grief arriving all at once, often at an inconvenient moment.

What willingness means

Willingness is a concept from acceptance-based approaches to psychology. It means being open to having a feeling, even a painful one, without needing it to go away first. It is not the same as liking the pain or wanting it. It is closer to saying: this hurts, and I am willing to let it hurt because it is connected to someone I love.

What willingness looks like day to day

  • Letting yourself cry in the car instead of holding it until you are home
  • Looking at a photo even though it stings
  • Saying their name in conversation
  • Answering honestly when someone asks how you are
  • Setting aside ten minutes to think about them on purpose

Small acts like these let grief move instead of building up behind a wall.

Willingness is not wallowing

People sometimes worry that letting grief in means getting swallowed by it. In practice, the opposite tends to happen. When feelings are allowed, they usually rise, peak, and fall. It is the fight against them that keeps them stuck. Willingness also includes being willing to take breaks: to laugh, watch a show, or think about something else without guilt.

Connecting to what matters

Willingness works best when it is tied to your values. Ask yourself what kind of person you want to be while grieving, and what this person meant to you. Maybe you want to stay close to your kids, keep their garden going, or be honest with friends. Pain becomes more bearable when it is in service of something that matters.

Start small

Choose one moment this week to lean toward grief instead of away. Write down one memory. Tell one story. Notice what happens. If grief ever feels like more than you can carry safely, call or text 988 for support any time.
