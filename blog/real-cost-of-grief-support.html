The Real Cost of Grief Support: Counseling, Groups, and Apps

The Murido TeamSep 23, 2026 · 5 min read

When you are grieving, the last thing you want to think about is money. But for many people, cost is a real barrier to getting support. You might wonder whether you can afford counseling, whether support groups are free, or whether an app is worth paying for.

This guide walks through the main types of grief support and what tends to affect their cost, so you can make a choice that fits your needs and your budget. Prices vary widely depending on where you live, your insurance, and the provider, so we focus on what drives cost rather than specific numbers. The goal is to help you find support without adding financial stress to an already heavy time.

Why cost matters in grief

A death can bring financial strain. There may be funeral expenses, lost income, medical bills, or the cost of settling an estate. At the same time, grieving people may need support more than ever.

Thinking about cost is not selfish or shallow. It is practical. Knowing your options can help you find something sustainable rather than starting support you cannot continue.

Good grief support does not have to be the most expensive kind. It has to be the kind you can keep showing up for.

Individual grief counseling or therapy

One on one counseling with a licensed professional is often the most personalized form of support. It can be especially helpful for complicated, traumatic, or prolonged grief.

What affects the cost

  • Credentials. Fees may differ between psychologists, licensed counselors, social workers, and other providers.
  • Location. Prices tend to be higher in large cities.
  • Insurance. Some plans cover therapy, though coverage varies. Grief alone may not always be covered unless another condition is diagnosed.
  • Session length and frequency. Weekly sessions cost more over time than every other week.
  • Format. Video sessions are sometimes less expensive than in person.

Ways to lower the cost

  1. Check your insurance. Call your plan and ask about mental health benefits and in network providers.
  2. Ask about sliding scale fees. Many therapists reduce fees based on income.
  3. Look into community mental health centers, which often offer lower cost services.
  4. Contact training clinics at universities, where supervised trainees may offer reduced fees.
  5. Use employee assistance programs. Many employers offer a limited number of free counseling sessions.

Hospice bereavement services

Many hospices offer bereavement support to families after a death. This may include counseling, support groups, mailings, and check in calls.

Hospice bereavement services are often free or low cost, especially if your loved one received hospice care. Some hospices extend services to the wider community, even if the person who died was not in their care. It is worth calling local hospices to ask what they offer.

Grief support groups

Support groups bring together people who have experienced similar losses. They may be led by a trained facilitator or by peers.

Cost

Many support groups are free or ask for a small donation. They may be offered by:

  • Hospices.
  • Hospitals.
  • Places of worship.
  • Community centers.
  • Nonprofit organizations focused on specific losses, such as child loss or suicide loss.

Some professionally led groups charge a fee, especially if they run for a set number of weeks.

What groups offer

Groups can provide a sense of belonging and understanding that is hard to find elsewhere. Hearing others share similar experiences can make your own grief feel less isolating. Our guide to grief support options explains how groups compare to other options.

Faith based support

Many faith communities offer support through clergy, chaplains, or lay ministers. This support is often free for members, and sometimes for anyone in the community. It may include one on one conversations, prayer, rituals, and support groups.

If your faith has been shaken by grief, this kind of support may feel complicated. Our guide to when grief shakes your faith explores this.

Online support

Online options have grown a great deal. These may include:

  • Online therapy platforms, which connect you with licensed counselors by video, phone, or text. Some use subscription pricing.
  • Online support groups, often free, sometimes hosted by nonprofits.
  • Forums and communities, usually free, where grieving people share experiences.

Online support can be convenient and sometimes less expensive. It can also connect you with people who share a very specific kind of loss.

Grief apps

Grief apps offer tools such as journaling prompts, guided reflections, educational content, memory keeping, or conversations designed to support grieving people. Pricing models vary. Some apps are free, some offer a free trial followed by a subscription, and some charge a one time fee.

Apps can be a helpful supplement to other support, especially for moments between therapy sessions or at times when other help is not available, like late at night. They are not a replacement for professional care when grief is severe or when someone is at risk of harm.

When looking at an app, you might consider:

  1. What features are included, and what is behind a paywall.
  2. How your data and privacy are handled.
  3. Whether the content feels supportive and accurate.
  4. Whether you can cancel easily.

Books and educational resources

Books, podcasts, and articles about grief are often inexpensive or free through libraries. Many people find comfort in reading about other people's experiences. Educational resources can also help you understand what you are feeling. You can start with our guide to what grief is.

Peer support and friends

Some of the most meaningful support costs nothing at all: friends who listen, family who remember, neighbors who check in. This support may not replace professional help when it is needed, but it is an important part of healing. Our guide to supporting a grieving loved one can be shared with people who want to help.

Choosing what fits

When deciding on support, it may help to ask:

  • What do I need most right now? Someone to talk to, others who understand, practical tools, or professional treatment?
  • How intense is my grief? If you are struggling to function, professional support may be the priority.
  • What can I afford over time? Consistent, affordable support often helps more than a short burst of expensive care.
  • What is available near me? Local options, like hospices and community groups, may be free.

You can also combine options. For example, you might attend a free support group, use a journaling app, and see a counselor once a month.

When cost should not stop you

If you are in crisis, cost should never stop you from getting help. In the United States, you can call or text 988 at any time for free, confidential support. Emergency rooms and crisis lines are there for moments when you feel unsafe.

Support that fits your life

Grief support comes in many forms and at many price points. The best support is the one you can access consistently and that helps you feel less alone. You do not need to spend a lot to grieve well. You need people, tools, and care that meet you where you are.
