Grief After a Long Illness: Anticipatory Grief and Caregiving

The Murido TeamAug 9, 2026 · 3 min read

When someone you love has a long illness, grief often begins long before they die. You grieve the person they were, the plans you had, and the losses that come with each stage of decline. This is called anticipatory grief.

What anticipatory grief feels like

  • Sadness and longing while they are still alive
  • Exhaustion from caregiving
  • Guilt for feeling impatient or wishing it would end
  • Fear of the death itself
  • Moments of closeness and meaning alongside the pain

It does not replace later grief

Some people expect that grieving ahead of time will make the death easier. Sometimes it does a little. But many caregivers are surprised by how hard the grief hits after death, along with the sudden loss of a caregiving role that filled their days.

Relief and guilt

After a long illness, relief is common: relief that their suffering ended, and relief from caregiving. This relief often brings guilt. Both feelings can be true at once. Relief does not mean you did not love them.

Memories of illness

Memories of the illness can crowd out memories of the healthy person. Deliberately recalling earlier years, through photos, stories, and conversations with others, can help bring the whole person back.

Caring for the caregiver

If you are caregiving now:

  • Accept help, even if it feels hard
  • Take short breaks without guilt
  • Talk openly with the person, if possible, about what matters to them
  • Record their stories and voice while you can

After the death

Give yourself time to rest and grieve both the person and the role. Caregiver support groups often include bereaved caregivers. If exhaustion or grief leads to feeling hopeless, call or text 988.
