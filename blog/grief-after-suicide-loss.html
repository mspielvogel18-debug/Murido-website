Kinds of Loss

Grief After Suicide Loss

July 31, 2026 · 3 min read · The Murido Team

If you lost someone to suicide, please know first: it was not your fault. Grief after suicide is often called complicated, not because you are grieving wrong, but because it carries so many extra layers.

The questions

Why? It is the question that echoes loudest. Survivors often search for reasons in texts, notes, and memories. Sometimes partial answers come. Often they do not. Suicide usually results from many factors, including illness and pain that the person may have hidden.

Guilt and blame

Survivors frequently feel they should have seen it coming or done something to stop it. Remember that people in crisis often hide their pain, and that even trained professionals cannot always predict suicide.

Stigma and silence

Others may not know what to say, avoid the subject, or say hurtful things. You may feel pressure to hide the cause of death. You have the right to share as much or as little as you want.

Anger and other feelings

Anger at the person, at yourself, at doctors, at the world is common. So is relief, if the person was suffering for a long time. All of these can coexist with deep love.

Remembering the whole person

Suicide can overshadow everything else about someone's life. Over time, many survivors find comfort in remembering who the person was beyond their death: their humor, kindness, and stories.

Support for survivors

There are support groups specifically for suicide loss survivors, and they can be a lifeline. Speaking with people who truly understand can ease isolation.

Your own safety

People bereaved by suicide are at higher risk of suicidal thoughts themselves. If you are having those thoughts, please call or text 988 right now. You matter, and help is available at any hour.
