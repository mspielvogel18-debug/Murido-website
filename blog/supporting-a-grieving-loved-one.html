Supporting a Grieving Loved One: A Guide for Family and Friends

October 11, 2026 · 3 min read · The Murido Team

When someone you love is grieving, you may desperately want to help and have no idea how. This guide offers practical ways to show up.

Say something

Silence often hurts more than imperfect words. Try: "I am so sorry. I am thinking of you." Or: "I don't know what to say, but I'm here."

Say their name

Many grieving people long to hear the person's name. Share a memory. Ask about them. It shows the person is not forgotten.

Avoid common pitfalls

  • "Everything happens for a reason"
  • "They are in a better place"
  • "At least..."
  • "I know exactly how you feel"
  • Pushing them to move on

These can feel dismissive even when meant kindly.

Offer specific help

Instead of "Let me know if you need anything," try "Can I bring dinner Tuesday?" or "I'm going to the store. What can I pick up?"

Show up over time

Support often fades after a few weeks. Put reminders in your calendar for months later and for birthdays and anniversaries. A simple text on a hard date can mean a great deal.

Listen more than you talk

You do not need to fix anything. Listening, being present, and allowing tears is often the most helpful thing.

Watch for warning signs

If your loved one talks about not wanting to live, cannot function for a long period, or relies heavily on alcohol or drugs, encourage professional help. If they are in danger, help them contact 988 or emergency services.
