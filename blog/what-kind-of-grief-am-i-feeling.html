Kinds of Loss

What Kind of Grief Am I Feeling?

The Murido TeamJul 10, 2026 · 3 min read

When you are grieving, it can help to have words for what is happening. Grief researchers and counselors use several terms to describe different kinds of grief. You might recognize more than one.

Acute grief

The intense grief of the early weeks and months after a loss. It often includes shock, longing, crying, and difficulty functioning.

Integrated grief

Grief that has become part of your life without dominating it. You still miss the person and have hard days, but you can engage with the present and imagine a future.

Anticipatory grief

Grief that begins before a death, often during a long illness or decline. Caregivers frequently feel it. It can bring guilt, especially if you find yourself wishing the suffering would end.

Disenfranchised grief

Grief that others do not recognize or validate. Examples include the death of an ex, a pet, a coworker, a pregnancy loss, or a relationship others disapproved of.

Ambiguous grief

Grief for someone who is physically present but psychologically gone, as in dementia, or someone who is missing without a clear answer.

Prolonged grief

Grief that remains intense, constant, and impairing for a long time, typically a year or more for adults. It is treatable.

Collective grief

Grief shared by a community or society after a public tragedy, disaster, or pandemic.

Why naming helps

Naming your grief can make it feel less chaotic and more understood. It can also help you find the right kind of support. Someone with disenfranchised grief may need validation most. Someone with anticipatory grief may need help with caregiver guilt.

Not a label to wear

These terms are tools, not boxes. Your grief is yours, and it may not fit neatly anywhere. Whatever kind you feel, it deserves care. If you are struggling to stay safe, call or text 988.
