What Actually Helps With Grief: Approaches That Work

September 5, 2026 · 3 min read · The Murido Team

There is no single cure for grief, but there are approaches that consistently help people carry it. Here is what research and the experience of grieving people suggest.

Social support

Having people who listen, say the name, and stay present over time is one of the strongest protections in grief. Quality matters more than quantity. One or two understanding people can make a big difference.

Expressing grief

Talking, writing, crying, art, and ritual all give grief somewhere to go. Expressive writing about loss has been studied for decades and many people find it helpful.

Continuing bonds

Staying connected to the person through memories, rituals, and inner conversations is now widely seen as a healthy part of grief for many people.

Balancing loss and life

The dual process model suggests people do best when they move between facing grief and engaging with daily life. Both sides matter.

Caring for the body

Sleep, food, and movement affect mood and resilience. Small steps count.

Support groups

Groups let people share with others who understand. Loss-specific groups, such as for parents, partners, or suicide loss, can be especially powerful.

Specialized therapy

For prolonged grief, therapies designed for it have good evidence. For trauma after a death, trauma-focused therapy helps.

What usually does not help

Suppressing grief for long periods, isolating, relying on alcohol or drugs, and holding yourself to a deadline tend to make things harder.

Finding your mix

Most people use a combination. Experiment and keep what helps. If nothing seems to help and you feel unsafe, call or text 988.
